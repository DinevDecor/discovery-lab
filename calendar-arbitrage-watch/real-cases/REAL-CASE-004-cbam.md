## Observed clock

CBAM is in its definitive regime from 2026, with certificate sales beginning in 2027 and later surrender/declaration obligations tied to 2026 imports.

Primary source: European Commission Taxation and Customs Union — CBAM definitive regime.
Source URL: https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/cbam-definitive-regime_en

## Calendar-arbitrage hypothesis

The scarce function may not be filing software itself but obtaining reliable, acceptable emissions data from non-EU producers in time for importer obligations. If that evidence-acquisition/verification capacity is difficult to secure and lead times rise, early contracting could create a calendar position.

## Current evidence boundary

Observed:
- dated regulatory regime and compliance obligations.

Not yet established:
- whether emissions-data acquisition is the dominant bottleneck;
- whether verification capacity is scarce;
- lead times and queue depth;
- willingness to pay for early access;
- defensibility versus commodity compliance software.

## Cheapest kill test

Test whether importers can already obtain the required emissions data and verification through abundant incumbent providers at short lead times. If yes, the calendar thesis weakens. If software is abundant but verified supplier data remains the binding constraint, continue.

## Method discipline

Do not substitute a plausible story for measured scarcity. No G_r, G_d, S_ready, RI or lifecycle promotion is asserted without evidenced inputs. INSUFFICIENT_DATA is not falsification.
