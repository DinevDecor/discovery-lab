# REAL-CASE-006 — Bulgaria data-center grid-capacity shock

Status: WATCH — HIGH-SIGNAL DEMAND SHOCK / ALLOCATION RULE UNRESOLVED
As of: 2026-08-17
Category: infrastructure_clock
Mode: ARCHAEOLOGY

## Question

Does Bulgaria's emerging data-center demand shock create a genuine Powered Land / Calendar Arbitrage opportunity around scarce transmission capacity, and if so, what part of the advantage is actually defensible: queue position, project readiness, anti-speculation compliance, ownership/control of connection infrastructure, or willingness to finance network reinforcement?

## New demand evidence

1. Bulgaria's Ministry of Energy stated that from the beginning of 2026 ESO had received investment applications for data centers with a combined requested power of nearly 9 GW. The same statement compares this with nine units the size of Kozloduy NPP blocks, making the scale of the requested load explicit.
Primary source: https://www.me.government.bg/bg/interviews-detail-type-506-315-.html

2. ESO published a 2026-2035 transmission-network development plan in March 2026, confirming that major network expansion planning is active while new large loads are arriving.
Primary source: https://www.eso.bg/doc?messages=928

## Allocation / scarcity evidence

3. Ordinance No. 6 allows a network operator to refuse full or partial connection of a consumption object at the requested terms when connection is technically impossible in the requested commissioning window, or where the connection would worsen transfer conditions to/from other objects because the necessary capacity is absent or exceeded. The operator must state the reasons and the measures needed to remove the constraint.
Primary source: https://dv.parliament.bg/DVWeb/showMaterialDV.jsp?idMat=211280

4. Connection conditions explicitly depend on requested power, the transfer capability of the existing network, and network-development plans. Therefore requested MW is not treated as automatically available capacity.
Primary source: https://dv.parliament.bg/DVWeb/showMaterialDV.jsp?idMat=211280

5. For renewable generation and storage projects, Bulgaria already uses BGN 50,000/MW guarantees intended to prevent speculative reservation of grid capacity by actors without genuine investment intent. This is not evidence that the same guarantee applies to data-center loads, but it is an authoritative anti-speculation precedent for scarce connection capacity.
KEVR source: https://www.dker.bg/news/1142/65/kevr-prie-okonchatelno-izmeneniya-i-doplneniya-v-naredba-6-za-prisedinyavane-na-obekti-km-elektricheskite-mrezhi/d%2Cnews-detail.html

6. ESO's renewable-connection registry records application date, requested and approved capacity, legal admissibility, and decisions. This proves that chronological and procedural state is observable in at least one major connection regime, but does not by itself prove first-come-first-served priority for large consumption loads.
Primary source: https://www.eso.bg/doc?news=611

## Readiness evidence

7. Bulgarian connection procedure uses real project-readiness gates. For consumption projects, the applicant must establish rights over the site at the application stage, and later contractual stages require the relevant approved investment project / construction permit where legally required. Those are progression gates, not yet evidence of an explicit comparative readiness score between competing applicants.
Primary source: https://dv.parliament.bg/DVWeb/showMaterialDV.jsp?idMat=211280

8. For a non-household customer, high- and medium-voltage installations serving only that customer may be designed and built at the customer's expense and remain its property. This matters because a large load can internalize part of the connection build instead of waiting for every connection component to be network-operator funded.
Primary sources: Energy Act art. 117(5), reflected in Ordinance No. 6; https://dv.parliament.bg/DVWeb/showMaterialDV.jsp?idMat=211280

## Evidence against an over-strong claim

9. The reviewed authoritative public rules do NOT establish a general Transgrid-style rule under which two otherwise admissible large-load projects competing for one constrained transmission node are ranked by financing secured, permits obtained, equipment ordered, or an overall project-credibility score.

10. Bulgarian law does contain an explicit priority rule in a different context: certain modernization requests for renewable generation are reviewed with priority over other connection requests. The existence of a specific statutory priority rule there is evidence that priority can be legislated explicitly; it does not justify inferring an unwritten equivalent for data centers.
Primary source: amendments to the Renewable Energy Act / connection framework: https://dv.parliament.bg/DVWeb/showMaterialDV.jsp?idMat=200211

## Current verdict

**REAL CALENDAR / CAPACITY SIGNAL: YES.**

The nearly 9 GW of announced data-center connection demand is a material demand shock relative to Bulgaria's existing power system, and Ordinance No. 6 confirms that actual transfer capability can constrain, delay, partially refuse, or require network-development measures for new consumption loads.

**TRANSGrid-STYLE READINESS ALLOCATION: NOT ESTABLISHED.**

No authoritative public source reviewed establishes that better-financed, better-permitted, or more construction-ready data-center projects automatically outrank earlier or competing technically admissible applications at the same constrained node.

**POWERED LAND THESIS: PLAUSIBLE BUT THE DEFENSIBLE ASSET IS NOT YET IDENTIFIED.**

The potential scarce asset could be one or more of:

- an advanced and still-valid connection procedure;
- a location at a node with demonstrable spare transfer capability;
- site/control rights plus completed permitting that shorten the path to a binding connection agreement;
- privately funded HV/MV connection infrastructure for a single non-household load;
- a contractual position tied to specific network reinforcement;
- or another ESO allocation right not yet visible in the public source horizon.

The evidence does NOT yet justify treating a bare land parcel near a substation as "Powered Land".

## Critical unresolved question

For two or more technically admissible large consumption requests competing for insufficient capacity at the same transmission node, what exact rule determines which project receives the scarce capacity first, and under what conditions can an existing reservation / contractual position be lost, reduced, transferred, or overtaken?

A written inquiry to ESO has been sent to resolve this question. Until a primary answer or an authoritative methodology is obtained, record the allocation mechanism as:

`NOT_FOUND_IN_CURRENT_SOURCE_HORIZON`

not:

`DOES_NOT_EXIST`.
