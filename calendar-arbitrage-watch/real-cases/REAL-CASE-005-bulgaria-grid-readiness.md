# REAL-CASE-005 — Bulgaria constrained-node grid readiness

Status: WATCH — KILL TEST PARTIAL
As of: 2026-08-17
Category: infrastructure_clock

## Question

When two or more projects compete for scarce connection capacity at a constrained Bulgarian grid node, does ESO/KEVR allocate that scarce capacity by project readiness/credibility (permits, financing, construction milestones, equipment commitments), rather than merely technical admissibility and procedural progress?

## Authoritative evidence found

1. KEVR's current Ordinance No. 6 (28 March 2024, amended through 14 April 2026) governs connection procedures and requires network operators to determine connection conditions using the transfer capability of the existing grid and network-development plans.
Primary source: https://dv.parliament.bg/DVWeb/showMaterialDV.jsp?idMat=211280

2. For storage projects, a BGN 50,000/MW guarantee is required after a connection opinion. KEVR states the purpose is to prevent speculative reservation of grid capacity by parties without genuine investment intent.
Primary source: https://www.dker.bg/news/1142/65/kevr-prie-okonchatelno-izmeneniya-i-doplneniya-v-naredba-6-za-prisedinyavane-na-obekti-km-elektricheskite-mrezhi/d%2Cnews-detail.html
ESO implementation notice: https://www.eso.bg/doc?messages=733

3. A similar BGN 50,000/MW guarantee exists for renewable generators in the connection procedure.
Primary source: https://www.eso.bg/doc?messages=594

4. ESO maintains an electronic registry containing application date, requested/allowed capacity, legal admissibility, and decisions for renewable connection applications.
Primary source: https://www.eso.bg/doc?news=611

5. The 2026 Ordinance No. 6 amendments add shared use of network transfer capability, showing that scarce capacity is actively managed, not treated as unlimited.
Primary source: https://www.dker.bg/news/1340/65/kevr-prie-okonchatelno-promenite-v-naredba-6-za-prisedinyavane-na-obekti-km-elektricheskite-mrezhi/d%2Cnews-detail.html

## Kill-test result

**PARTIAL SUPPORT, STRONG CLAIM NOT ESTABLISHED.**

Bulgarian rules clearly use readiness-like gates and anti-speculation guarantees. They also require technical feasibility and account for grid transfer capability. However, in the authoritative public materials reviewed, no general rule was found that ranks competing otherwise-valid projects at the same constrained node by financing, permits, equipment procurement, or overall project-credibility score.

Therefore the strong Powered-Land claim — "the more financeable/permitted/build-ready project wins scarce MW" — is NOT established for Bulgaria by this review.

## What appears to be the operative public rule

The public framework is procedural + technical rather than an explicit Transgrid-style credibility ranking:

- submit a valid connection request;
- network operator studies technical feasibility and available transfer capability;
- applicant progresses through opinion / preliminary agreement / connection agreement as applicable;
- guarantees can be required to keep capacity from being reserved speculatively;
- permits/approved investment project become necessary gates for later contractual/progress stages;
- shared-capacity arrangements are possible under the 2026 amendments.

Whether chronological position in the application queue has legal priority at a constrained node, and how ESO resolves simultaneous/competing admissible large-load requests when capacity is insufficient, was **NOT FOUND IN THE CURRENT PUBLIC SOURCE HORIZON**. This is intentionally not recorded as "no such rule exists."
