# REAL-CASE-002 — EU Batteries due-diligence verification capacity

Status: WATCH — REAL CASE INTAKE
As of: 2026-08-17
Category: regulatory_clock
Mode: DISCOVERY

## Observed clock

EU battery due-diligence obligations were postponed to 18 August 2027. The legislative rationale explicitly cites slower-than-expected designation of notified bodies and lack of recognized due-diligence schemes as implementation constraints.

Primary source: EUR-Lex consolidated Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 and 2025 amendment context.
Source URL: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX:02023R1542-20250731

## Calendar-arbitrage hypothesis

If third-party verification / recognized-scheme capacity remains scarce as the 2027 date approaches, early access to qualified verification capacity may become a scarce calendar position.

This is a hypothesis, not an established opportunity.

## Current evidence boundary

Observed:
- dated regulatory obligation;
- official postponement;
- official acknowledgement of verification/scheme-readiness constraints.

Not yet established:
- present notified-body capacity;
- queue length;
- lead time;
- ability to reserve or contract capacity early;
- economic value of early positioning;
- competitor readiness.

## Cheapest kill test

Determine whether sufficient recognized/notified verification capacity is already available or credibly scheduled well ahead of 18 August 2027. If capacity is abundant and booking lead times are short, the calendar-arbitrage thesis is killed or materially weakened.

## Method discipline

No G_r, G_d, S_ready, RI, latest_safe_date, lifecycle promotion, or numeric scoring is asserted until the corresponding inputs are independently evidenced. Missing inputs remain INSUFFICIENT_DATA; they are not negative evidence.
