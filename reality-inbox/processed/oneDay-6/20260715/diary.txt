GRIF:
id: KOD-RES-BOUNDARY-GVS-001
project: KOD
category: Research
subtype: Boundary Conditions
title: Generation–Validation Separation Boundary Conditions
state: RESEARCH
confidence: MEDIUM
priority: HIGH
maturity: CONVERGENCE
owner: Petko Dinev

purpose: >
Determine under which conditions separating generation from
validation produces legitimate outcomes, and under which
conditions the separation breaks down.

research_question: >
When does a minimal, deterministic validator increase trust,
and when does it merely create an illusion of legitimacy?

candidate_principle: >
Whenever a system must produce legitimate outputs,
free generation should be separated from validation,
and legitimacy should be conferred only by a minimal,
constrained, externally inspectable validation layer.

status: >
This is a research hypothesis supported by convergence across
multiple independent domains. It is NOT yet a validated principle.

convergence_evidence:

- LCF theorem proving
- Trusted Computing Base (security engineering)
- Constitutional rule-of-law systems
- KOD Kernel architecture
- AI Agent OS policy-kernel architecture

hypothesis:

If the validator remains:
  - minimal
  - deterministic where possible
  - externally inspectable
  - governed by explicit contracts
  - slow to change
then trust becomes concentrated on a small auditable surface.
Intelligence remains outside the trusted core.

expected_benefit:

The architecture does not eliminate the trust problem.
It compresses trust into a small, governable, auditable surface.

predicted_failure_conditions:

FC-001:
  Criteria cannot be externalized or formalized.
FC-002:
  Validation depends entirely on subjective judgement.
FC-003:
  Contract authors become captured or corrupted.
FC-004:
  Generators optimize for passing validation
  rather than for truth or reality (Goodhart pressure).
FC-005:
  Validator becomes large, adaptive,
  opaque or self-modifying.
FC-006:
  Authority chain is ambiguous or disputed.

predictions:

- Systems following this architecture should exhibit
  higher auditability.
- They should be easier to govern over time.
- Failures should increasingly originate in contract
  governance rather than validator implementation.
- Trust failures should cluster around boundary
  conditions rather than kernel mechanics.

next_research:

- Classify validation criteria into formal vs semantic.
- Determine which semantic criteria can be made
  mechanically verifiable.
- Model governance of contract evolution.
- Study adversarial optimization against validators.
- Investigate whether multi-reviewer architectures
  reduce semantic validation uncertainty.

open_question: >
Is Generation–Validation Separation a domain-specific design
pattern, or a fundamental architectural law governing systems
that must produce legitimate beliefs or legitimate actions?

success_criterion: >
The hypothesis becomes a validated KOD principle only if its
boundary conditions are mapped, its predictions repeatedly hold
across independent domains, and no simpler architecture explains
the same observations equally well.

Това ми харесва повече от формулировка тип „нов закон“, защото е напълно в духа на KOD: първо дефинираме хипотезата, после търсим границите, при които тя се проваля. Ако оцелее след тези тестове, едва тогава заслужава да стане принцип.
